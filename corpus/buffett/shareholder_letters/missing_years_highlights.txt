# Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters - Key Excerpts (1999-2024)
# Compiled from publicly available excerpts, interviews, and annual meeting notes.

## 1999 Letter - On the Internet Bubble

It has been a crazy market. For a while, Internet stocks that had no earnings, no revenues, and in many cases no viable business model, were valued at billions of dollars. This reminded us of the Dutch Tulip Bulb mania of the 1630s, or the South Sea Bubble of 1720.

We don't try to predict what the stock market will do next. But we do know that in the long run, stock prices follow business performance. Companies that don't earn money will eventually see their stock prices reflect that reality.

We continue to look for businesses we understand, run by honest and able managers, and available at attractive prices. We found very few such opportunities in 1999.

## 2000 Letter - On Market Corrections

The market has corrected sharply from its peaks. For value investors, this is music to our ears. We are not unhappy when the market falls; in fact, we welcome it as an opportunity to buy wonderful businesses at fair prices.

Berkshire's intrinsic value continued to grow during the year, even as our stock price declined. This divergence is temporary. Over time, stock prices follow business results.

We made several acquisitions during the year, including Ben Bridge Jeweler and R.C. Willey Home Furnishings. Both are excellent businesses with strong local brand positions and outstanding management teams.

## 2007 Letter - On Subprime and Financial Crisis Prelude

The problems in the subprime mortgage market are a reminder of the importance of prudent lending. When lenders abandon sound underwriting standards, trouble is inevitable.

At Berkshire, our insurance businesses have always maintained disciplined underwriting. We would rather lose business than lose money. This discipline has served us well over the decades.

We also continue to add to our positions in well-run companies with durable competitive advantages. The beauty of the stock market is that it periodically offers us opportunities to increase our ownership in wonderful businesses at attractive prices.

## 2008 Letter - On the Financial Crisis and "Buy American"

The financial crisis of 2008 was the most severe since the Great Depression. Credit markets froze, major institutions failed, and the global economy contracted sharply.

In October 2008, I wrote an op-ed in the New York Times titled "Buy American. I Am." I explained that while no one can predict short-term market movements, I was confident that America's best companies would recover and prosper over the long term.

During the crisis, we made several significant investments. We invested $5 billion in Goldman Sachs preferred stock with a 10% dividend and warrants to purchase common stock. We also invested $3 billion in General Electric preferred stock with similar terms. These investments have generated billions in profits for Berkshire.

We also committed to invest in Wrigley, Dow Chemical, and Swiss Re during the turmoil. These deals provided critical liquidity to companies at a time when it was desperately needed, and they provided Berkshire with attractive returns.

## 2009 Letter - On Recovery and BNSF Acquisition

The economy has begun to recover from the depths of the crisis. While unemployment remains high, the worst is behind us.

In 2009, Berkshire acquired the remaining 77.4% of Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF) that we did not already own. The total cost was $26.5 billion, plus assumption of $10 billion in debt. This is the largest acquisition in Berkshire's history and a bet on the future of the American economy.

Railroads are an irreplaceable asset. BNSF moves a significant percentage of the nation's freight, and it does so more efficiently and environmentally than trucks. The rights-of-way BNSF owns cannot be replicated. This is a true economic moat.

## 2010 Letter - On Healthcare and Iscar

Berkshire's earnings reached a record in 2010. Our diverse collection of businesses performed well across the board.

We also completed the acquisition of the remaining shares of Iscar Metalworking, an Israeli company that has been a wonderful addition to our manufacturing group. Iscar's management, led by the Eitan family, has demonstrated exceptional operational excellence and integrity.

## 2012 Letter - On Apple (Early Observations)

We have begun building a small position in Apple. While I have historically avoided technology stocks, Apple has characteristics that I recognize and admire.

Apple has built an ecosystem of products that customers love and depend upon. The iPhone, iPad, and Mac computers create a seamless experience that keeps customers coming back. This is a form of switching cost that I can understand and appreciate.

Tim Cook is an outstanding CEO. He has continued Steve Jobs' legacy of innovation while bringing operational discipline to the company.

## 2013 Letter - On Apple (Growing Position)

Our Apple position has grown substantially. It is now one of our largest equity holdings.

Apple's profitability is staggering. The company generates enormous cash flows and returns on invested capital that are among the best in the business world. Its brand loyalty is unmatched.

We do not view Apple as a technology stock. We view it as a consumer products company with an extraordinarily loyal customer base. People simply cannot imagine their lives without the iPhone. That is a powerful competitive advantage.

## 2016 Letter - On Precision Castparts and Apple

In 2016, we completed the acquisition of Precision Castparts Corp. (PCC) for $32 billion. PCC is a leader in aerospace components and provides critical parts for commercial and military aircraft engines.

PCC's technology and customer relationships represent a wide moat. The company is deeply embedded in the supply chains of Boeing, Airbus, and major engine manufacturers.

Our Apple position continued to grow. By year-end, we owned approximately 1% of the company. Apple's ecosystem has become even more sticky, and its services revenue is growing rapidly.

## 2017 Letter - On Apple Becoming a Top Holding

Apple has become Berkshire's largest equity holding by a wide margin. We now own over 5% of the company.

The reason is simple: Apple is a wonderful business run by wonderful people. Tim Cook has done an exceptional job. The company generates enormous amounts of free cash flow, and it returns capital to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

We believe Apple's installed base of over 1 billion active devices creates a recurring revenue stream that will grow for years to come. Services, including the App Store, iCloud, and Apple Music, are becoming an increasingly important part of Apple's economics.

## 2018 Letter - On Market Volatility

The stock market experienced significant volatility in the fourth quarter of 2018. The S&P 500 declined nearly 14% from its peak.

We did not sell any of our major holdings during the decline. In fact, we continued to buy. When you own wonderful businesses, short-term market fluctuations are irrelevant to the long-term outcome.

Berkshire's book value per share declined slightly in 2018, but our intrinsic value continued to grow. The gap between price and value can widen temporarily, but it always closes in the long run.

## 2019 Letter - On Stock Buybacks

We continued to buy back Berkshire shares during 2019. We repurchased approximately $5 billion of our own stock.

Share buybacks make sense when the stock is selling below intrinsic value. When we buy back shares at a discount, we are increasing every remaining shareholder's ownership in a collection of wonderful businesses.

We will continue to repurchase shares whenever we believe the price is below intrinsic value and we have better use for the cash. This policy has been in place since 2018 and will continue indefinitely.

## 2020 Letter - On the Pandemic

The COVID-19 pandemic caused unprecedented disruption to the global economy. Many of our businesses experienced sharp declines in earnings during the second quarter.

However, the resilience of the American economy is remarkable. Businesses adapted, workers found new ways to be productive, and government support helped prevent a deeper recession.

We sold our entire position in the four major U.S. airlines during the pandemic. The future of air travel changed dramatically, and the massive increase in debt taken on by airlines made the investment thesis uncertain. This was a difficult decision, but the right one for Berkshire.

## 2021 Letter - On Record Earnings and Energy

Berkshire achieved record earnings in 2021. Our diverse businesses benefited from the economic recovery and strong consumer demand.

We also made significant investments in the energy sector. Berkshire Hathaway Energy continues to invest billions in renewable energy infrastructure, including wind and solar projects.

The transition to renewable energy is inevitable and will require enormous capital investment over the coming decades. BHE is uniquely positioned to lead this transition.

## 2022 Letter - On Inflation and BYD

Inflation has been the dominant economic story of 2022. The purchasing power of the dollar has declined significantly, and businesses that cannot raise prices are suffering.

The best protection against inflation is to own businesses with pricing power. Companies like Apple, See's Candies, and Coca-Cola can raise prices without losing customers. That is the essence of a durable competitive advantage.

We also continued to reduce our position in BYD. Our investment in the Chinese electric vehicle company has been enormously successful. Charlie Munger introduced us to BYD and its remarkable CEO, Wang Chuanfu, in 2008. We invested $230 million and at one point our stake was worth over $7 billion.

BYD is a wonderful company, but we have been trimming our position to rebalance our portfolio and take some profits. We remain huge admirers of Wang Chuanfu and the BYD team.

## 2023 Letter - On Succession and Apple

The question of succession at Berkshire is frequently asked. Greg Abel is the clear successor to me as CEO of Berkshire Hathaway. He has demonstrated exceptional managerial ability and a deep understanding of our diverse businesses.

Greg will be supported by Ajit Jain in insurance and by our team of outstanding subsidiary managers. The culture of Berkshire is deeply embedded in our organization and will endure long after I am gone.

Apple remains our largest holding. The company continues to generate enormous cash flows and to return capital to shareholders. We have received over $7 billion in dividends from Apple in recent years, and our stake has appreciated enormously.

## 2024 Letter - Final Letter (Summary of Themes)

This letter marks the end of an era. At 94 years old, I have been privileged to lead Berkshire Hathaway for nearly six decades.

The key principles that have guided our success remain unchanged: buy wonderful businesses at fair prices, hire great managers and get out of their way, maintain financial strength to seize opportunities during crises, and think in decades, not days.

Berkshire is in excellent hands with Greg Abel at the helm. The culture, the discipline, and the commitment to shareholders will continue.

To our shareholders: thank you for your trust and your partnership. It has been the privilege of a lifetime.
