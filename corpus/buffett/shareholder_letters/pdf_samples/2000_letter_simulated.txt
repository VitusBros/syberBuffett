BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC.

                 To the Shareholders of Berkshire Hathaway Inc.:

     Our gain in net worth during 1999 was $10.5 billion, which increased the
per-share book value of both our Class A and Class B stock by 0.5%. Over the
last 35 years (that is, since present management took over) our per-share book
value has grown from $19 to $37,987, a rate of 23.6% compounded annually.

     The table below shows the year-by-year gains, the S&P 500 Index gains,
and the comparative advantage of Berkshire.

     What Charlie Munger and I look for in our managers is not just a
willingness to work hard but also a deep-seated love of the business. We want
people who run their businesses as if they own 100% of the family assets.

     We believe that when you find a truly wonderful business, you should hold
on to it. Time is the friend of the wonderful business and the enemy of the
mediocre.

     Our insurance business continues to be the cornerstone of Berkshire's
success. The key to our success is float. Float is money that we hold but
don't own. It comes from premiums paid in advance of losses paid out.

     If our insurance business breaks even over time, we have essentially
obtained the use of this float for free. That's a huge advantage.

     Warren E. Buffett
     Chairman and Chief Executive Officer
